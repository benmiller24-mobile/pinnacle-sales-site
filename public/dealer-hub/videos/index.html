Welcome to this training on appliance cabinet cutouts with WW Wood Products cabinetry.

In this session, we'll walk through the different types of cutouts available,

the construction styles, and how to properly design and order them. Whether you're working

with Shiloh or Eclipse cabinetry, this guide will help you get it right the first time.

An appliance cutout simply means that a portion of the cabinet is modified to fit in appliance.

Common examples include ovens, built-in microwaves, and warming drawers.

The type of construction you choose determines how the appliance will sit in the cabinet.

In standard construction, the appliance opening is created using a 3 quarter inch overlay front

panel. The appliance sits in front of this panel. In Eclipse frameless construction, the same effect

can be achieved without an overlay panel, and the appliance will sit against the box of the cabinet

which will feel a bit more streamlined. Flush inset construction is different. It also uses an

overlay panel. But here, the cutout surrounds the outside of the appliance, so the front

of the appliance is flush with the face of the cabinet. This is the most built-in option

where the appliance will not be proud of the front of the cabinet. This flush-inset construction

can be used in both Shiloh Framed and Eclipse Frameless cabinet designs.

Types of Appliance Cabinets

Appliance cabinets are available in wall, base, and tall cabinet configurations, and

come in multiple depths—18, 21, 24—and in select utility oven cabinets, even 27 and

30 inches. These size options give you flexibility to match your appliance specifications.

Tall oven cabinets—universal

In Shiloh Cabinetry, you can choose universal oven cabinets, labeled OUF and OMUF. These

do not come with a specific cutout size. Instead, you modify them in the field. The height is

modified by removing drawer components and trimming up to 6.5 inches from the oven opening

header. Width adjustments are made by trimming and attaching the included 1.5 inch fillers.

The floor is a [thickness unclear] plywood piece installed in the field. Tall oven cabinets: standard

cutout. Standard cutout oven cabinets labeled O for single cutouts and OM for oven microwave

combinations are available in both Shiloh and Eclipse. In Eclipse, you can order them

with or without an overlay frame. With overlay frames, the cutout must be at least 3 inches

narrower than the cabinet width to allow for the frame stiles to each be at least 1.5

inches wide. For example, a 30-inch wide cabinet would not be able to have a cutout larger

than 27 inches wide. However, if you are using a cabinet without an overlay frame, the cabinet

width can often match the appliance width exactly. So, for example, a 30-inch wide cabinet

can house a 30-inch wide oven, overlay versus no overlay examples. Here you can see the

difference. With an overlay panel, a 30-inch wide oven is installed in a 33-inch wide oven

cabinet. On the right, without an overlay panel, the cabinet itself can be 30 inches

wide to match the appliance. Tall oven cabinets, flush inset. Flush inset oven cabinets are

available in both Shiloh and Eclipse. In these the outer frame surrounds the appliance, so

it sits flush with the cabinet face. Ordering requires careful attention. You must specify

both the outer overlay frame and the inner subframe dimensions on the flush inset cutout

sheet. Here we see two examples of flush inset oven cabinets in framed and frameless cabinetry.

The picture on the left shows a flush inset oven microwave cabinet in Shiloh inset construction

cabinetry. The picture on the right shows a flush inset oven cabinet in eclipse frameless

cabinetry. Wall Microwave Custom Cabinets. For wall microwave custom cabinets, standard

depths are 18, 21, and 24 inches with an additional 16-inch depth option in Eclipse. The interior

is always unfinished and you must include the exact cutout width and height on the cutout

sheet and include with your order. Base Appliance Cabinets. Base oven cabinets and base warming

drawer cabinets differ mainly in their lower storage. Base oven cabinets typically have

one drawer or no drawers if using a taller appliance. While warming drawer-based cabinets

will have two drawers. But the naming shouldn't limit you. You can use any built-in appliance

in either style. As an example, the most common appliance used on a base oven cabinet is a

drawer microwave. As with all appliance cabinets, remember to include all cutout dimensions

on your cutout sheet and include with your order. Here are a few examples. A standard

base oven cutout in Shiloh inset cabinetry, a standard tall oven microwave cabinet with

no overlay in eclipse cabinetry, and a wall microwave custom cabinet.

Order forms.

Every appliance cutout order must include the correct appliance cutout worksheet available

in the resources section of our dealer portal at my.wwinc.com.

This ensures accurate manufacturing and a perfect fit for your appliances.

By understanding these options and providing complete, accurate information on your order

forms, you can confidently design appliance cabinets that fit perfectly and look beautiful.

Thank you for watching and happy designing with WW wood products.

The channels themselves are fixed on the cabinets

and do not move when doors and drawers are opened.

This system eliminates the need for protruding handles.

And best of all, there's no drilling required

on the cabinet doors or drawers.

That means a cleaner, more minimal look

and easier maintenance over time.

For wall cabinets, the GOLA system is all about subtlety.

Unlike how the channel is visible on base cabinets and tall cabinets,

GOLA wall cabinets are designed without an exposed channel.

The eclipse cabinetry offers several options for GOLA wall cabinets.

The most simple and affordable option is to add a recessed bottom shelf

RBS modification to a standard eclipse wall cabinet.

This option requires no hardware at all.

The recessed bottom shelf creates an inch and a half high space behind each door, allowing hand access to open the door,

but also creating that clean, invisible look.

For a higher-tech alternative, Eclipse also offers a GOLA LED continuous pull option.

This involves a groove in the bottom shelf for the GWL-CPA LED strip.

However, please note that this pull is installed in the field and must be ordered separately.

Also keep in mind that the LED continuous pulls are only available in the aluminum finish.

Although they will be hidden behind any closed doors, they will be visible every time the

door is opened.

Your wall cabinet codes must also include FWC as a prefix when ordering so that the

cabinets are prepped at the factory with the groove to accept the LED strip.

For those who prefer a push-to-open experience, touch latches or tip-on hardware can be used.

This is an alternative way to achieve a handleless design without visible hardware or channels.

GOLA is most often used in base cabinets, and that's where you'll find the most options.

The Eclipse factory prepares all GOLA base cabinets to accept two styles of horizontal

GOLA channel rails.

The L channel is located at the top of the base cabinet, just under the countertop.

The C channel is placed between doors and drawers.

The channels come in 13 foot long pieces that must be ordered separately.

The channels are to be field installed across several cabinets and they can be cut to length

to end where the channel terminates in the design.

For example, at the end of a run of base cabinets where cabinets meet a wall or where a door

meets a door in the neighboring cabinet.

Also to know, all GOLA base cabinets use L channel, but not all will use C channel depending

on the configuration of doors and drawers in the cabinet.

Some tall and vanity cabinets can also incorporate the horizontal L and C channels, mirroring

the base cabinet configurations.

GOLA channels are available in six metallic finishes, white lacquer, anodized aluminum,

stainless steel, slate, matte black, and gold anodized.

L channels and C channels each have their own parts and accessories including corner

transition pieces and end caps.

Refer to the Eclipse book pages R40 through R46 for L channels and R43 through R45 for

C channels to ensure that you're using the right components in your design.
