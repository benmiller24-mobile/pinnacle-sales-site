Project Workflows

Five short lessons to help you feel ready for the whole kitchen, from acknowledgment to the final details.

Walk through these with your team before your first live order. Each has a short video, captions, a transcript and a practice example. The sample jobs are fictional; use the current factory guidance for your real projects.

  • Handle a delivery issue
  • Talk through natural wood variation
  • Keep a parts request moving

Check your factory acknowledgment

How this helps: Catch small differences early and keep the order moving in the right direction.

Pinnacle process lesson · September 2026 · Synthetic narration

Read the lesson transcript
  1. Have your latest submitted order packet and factory acknowledgment side by side. Start with the account, project, brand and ship-to address.
  2. Work through each line: quantity, cabinet code, dimensions, modifications, door, species, finish and separately ordered components. Check whether accessories are sold individually or in pairs or sets.
  3. If something looks different, mark it clearly and send the correction through the factory's process. Check the correction deadline and save a copy of your request.
  4. When the revised acknowledgment arrives, make sure it matches the requested change. For anything still open, note who is following up and when you'll check back.

Try it

Try it with TRAIN-001: your order packet lists two cabinets, but the acknowledgment lists one. What would you send to the factory, and what would you look for in the reply?

Talk it through

Send a clearly marked request for a quantity of two and keep a copy. Then check the revised acknowledgment for that quantity. If it still shows one, keep the item open and follow up.

Handle a delivery issue

How this helps: Give the service team a clear picture so they can help with the next step.

Pinnacle process lesson · September 2026 · Synthetic narration

Read the lesson transcript
  1. Match the delivery paperwork to the order and take a look at the shipment as it arrives.
  2. Note the order, affected item, quantity and what you see. Take a photo of the full item and a few close-ups that make the issue easy to understand.
  3. Add any receiving notes required by the factory or carrier, and report the issue promptly. Check their reporting instructions and deadlines while the details are fresh.
  4. Keep the paperwork, photos and replies together. Note who is helping and the next update. Continue tracking the kitchen through installation and any parts needed to finish the job.

Try it

For TRAIN-002, one drawer front arrives damaged. Think through what you would gather to help the service team understand the issue without several rounds of questions.

Talk it through

Start with the order and item details, quantity, receiving paperwork, a short description, and full-view and close-up photos. Check the reporting deadline, then keep the replies and next follow-up with the job.

Talk through natural wood variation

How this helps: Help customers understand their selection and get a clear answer when something needs a closer look.

Pinnacle process lesson · September 2026 · Synthetic narration

Read the lesson transcript
  1. Start with the current physical sample and the customer's approved species and finish. Keep the selection record handy so everyone is looking at the same starting point.
  2. Look at the actual pieces in consistent lighting. Photos are helpful for sharing a concern, but they can change how color appears.
  3. Use the factory's current guidance to talk through natural grain and color differences. Set expectations around the material while leaving room for the factory to review a specific concern.
  4. If you're unsure, gather clear photos and selection details for the factory team. Let the customer know what you're checking and when you'll update them, even if the answer is still pending.

Try it

A customer notices different grain on two natural-wood fronts. How would you walk through the concern before deciding whether the pieces need factory review?

Talk it through

Review the physical sample, approved selection, actual pieces, lighting and current factory guidance. If the answer is unclear, share the details with the factory and arrange a customer update. A website photo is only a starting point.

Keep a parts request moving

How this helps: Start with the right details and make the next step easy to follow.

Pinnacle process lesson · September 2026 · Synthetic narration

Read the lesson transcript
  1. Pull up the original order and item or cabinet details. Describe the exact part and how many you need.
  2. Use the factory's current request form and include any photos, video or delivery and installation records the request calls for.
  3. Ask the factory to confirm how the request will be handled, including any coverage, charges and timing. Share those details with the customer as they are confirmed.
  4. Your team prepares, submits and follows the routine request. Keep the factory's reply and next follow-up with the job. Bring your rep in when something is unusual, stuck, urgent or putting the customer relationship at risk.

Try it

TRAIN-003 needs a replacement hinge. Before sending the request, what would you add to make it easier for the factory to identify and supply the right part?

Talk it through

Include the original order, item or cabinet details, exact hinge and quantity, plus the current form and any requested photos. Ask the factory to confirm handling, coverage or charges, and timing, then set your next follow-up.

Finish each kitchen with confidence

How this helps: Build confidence through three complete kitchens, with your rep alongside you for coaching and review.

Pinnacle process lesson · September 2026 · Synthetic narration

Read the lesson transcript
  1. Keep the original order, related purchase orders and any replacements together under one kitchen project. That makes the full job easier to follow.
  2. Your team prepares the forms and submits the orders, including the first three kitchens. Your rep coaches and reviews with you through quoting, ordering, acknowledgment, delivery and installation.
  3. Once installation is complete, check that any parts or service work needed to finish the kitchen has been resolved. Keep the final updates with the job so everyone knows where things stand.
  4. After three separate, complete kitchens, talk with your rep about what feels comfortable and where more practice would help. Your rep confirms when you're ready to order independently, or helps you plan the next training step.

Try it

You have three shipped purchase orders for one kitchen, and installation is next. Where does that put you in the first-three-kitchens process?

Talk it through

You're still working through your first kitchen; none is complete yet. The related orders stay together as one project, and the cycle finishes after installation and any required parts work. After three complete kitchens, your rep confirms readiness for independent ordering.
